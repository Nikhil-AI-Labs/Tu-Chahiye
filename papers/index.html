What each prediction is betting on

the one thing per subject we would not skip

Every predicted paper comes with its reasoning written out on its own page. This is the compressed version — the single call that most affects how you revise, and how sure we are of it.

SubjectThe callConfidence
EC301Digital Comm 2025 broke the pattern and it will stay broken. The older papers were quantisation-and-entropy arithmetic; 2025 went waveform-and-block-diagram heavy. We have predicted the newer shape, which means drawing matters as much as calculating. High
EC321Networks The numerical core repeats almost verbatim. ALOHA throughput, CSMA/CD minimum frame size, bandwidth-delay product and sliding-window efficiency, CRC by long division — all three years, every year. Learn the four calculations cold and a third of the paper is already written. Very high
EC303Signals The DFT and its computational cost is the anchor question. Every paper we have asks you to take a small DFT by hand and then compare direct evaluation against the FFT in multiplications. The rest moves; that does not. High
EC341Image Proc Spatial filtering will appear, and it could not have appeared before. Lectures 21–23 — masks, median, Laplacian, Sobel — were taught after the only past paper was set. They are dense, calculable, and exactly this examiner’s style. Reasoned
C++Objects It will be a code paper, not a theory paper. Predict-the-output, find-the-errors, write-a-class. And inheritance — a whole new deck, never yet examined — is worth about a fifth of the marks. Unverified

confidence is ours, and it is honest: “very high” means the same question appeared in every paper we hold · “unverified” means there is no past paper at all to check against

How the predicting was done

so you can judge it for yourself

Read every scan, question by question

Not skimmed for topics — transcribed. Marks, sub-parts, the wording of the instruction line, which questions offered a choice. The shape of a paper carries as much information as its content, and it is the part that changes least from year to year.

Tabulate what repeats

Each subject’s page opens with a grid: topic down the side, year across the top, a filled square where it appeared. Rows that are filled all the way across are tinted. That table is doing the actual predicting — everything after it is interpretation.

Correct for what has been taught since

A pattern read alone will predict last year’s paper. Two subjects have moved: DIP has three new lectures of spatial filtering, and C++ has a whole inheritance deck. Both are on the syllabus, neither is in any past paper, and both are weighted accordingly.

Solve it, then check the numbers

Every predicted question is answered in full on the same page. Throughputs, frame sizes, DFTs, histogram tables, bit planes — each was computed independently rather than quoted, and where a scan is too faint to read, the page says so instead of inventing the question.

What this is not

read this bit
The predicted papers are a reading of a pattern. They are not a leak, and nobody has seen your paper. If a prediction is right it is because the same examiner sets the same kind of question every year, which is a real and useful regularity — not because we know anything.

Two more things worth being straight about. Some scans are unreadable. Where a question could not be made out, the page marks it as illegible rather than guessing at the wording — you will see that a few times in the older DSP and DComm papers. And one subject has no past paper at all. The C++ page says so at the top, in its own section, and explains exactly what its prediction is built from instead.

Use these the way you would use a senior’s solved copy: excellent for finding out whether you can actually do the arithmetic under time pressure, useless as a substitute for the notes. The notes are here →
